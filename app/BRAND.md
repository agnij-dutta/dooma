# VERDICT — Brand Bible

> *Night Court.* A crypto-native tribunal where an incorruptible machine settles human beef in public — and hands you the receipt.

This document is the source of truth for VERDICT's voice, story, and on-screen words. Designers build to it. The community quotes it. Every line here is meant to be pasted straight into the UI.

---

## 1. The Brand World & Premise

It's always midnight at VERDICT. The room is dark and warm as old ink, lit by a single spotlight on the bench. Two people walk in with a grievance — a bet, a debt, a "he said / she said" that's been festering — and they put their money where their mouth is: real funds, locked in escrow on 0G Chain, before a word is spoken. The crowd files into the gallery to take sides. Then the gavel drops. The judge isn't a person who can be flattered, paid off, or worn down. It's a machine sealed inside a cryptographically attested enclave — and when it rules, the escrow pays the winner automatically. No appeal to the management. No "the refs were bought." The court is the contract.

What makes VERDICT a *place* and not just a feature is that it's adversarial, public, and final — by design. Most of the internet argues forever because nothing is ever settled. Here, arguments **end**. Someone wins, someone loses, the money moves, and a verdict card is minted with a wax seal that reads "Verified by TEE." That card is the trophy and the receipt at once: schadenfreude for the gallery, vindication for the winner, and a provenance trail for the skeptic. VERDICT turns the oldest human ritual — taking your fight before a judge — into something a crypto-savvy crowd can actually trust, because the one thing they hate most is being told to trust.

And that's the spine of the whole world: **don't trust us — audit the court.** VERDICT never asks for faith. The judge's credentials are clickable. The proof is exportable. The honesty is the swagger. It's a tribunal that hands you the keys to the evidence room on your way out and dares you to find the rig. You won't — because there isn't one. That confidence, theatrical but earned, is the personality a designer can dress and a community can rally behind.

## 2. Voice & Tone

VERDICT speaks like a judge who's seen everything and is mildly amused by all of it. Confident, theatrical, precise. Gravitas with a raised eyebrow. It is never breathless, never salesy, and it never begs to be believed — it shows its work and lets you check.

**Six principles, with do / don't:**

**1. Judicial, not corporate.** Speak in rulings, verdicts, exhibits, and the record — not "solutions," "platforms," or "ecosystems."
- ✅ "The gavel drops at 9PM ET."
- ❌ "Our platform leverages cutting-edge AI to resolve disputes."

**2. Show the receipt, never claim faith.** Confidence comes from verifiability, not adjectives. Always point to the proof.
- ✅ "Don't trust us. Audit the court."
- ❌ "The most trustworthy AI judge ever built."

**3. Precise about what's proven — it's a flex, not a footnote.** We brag about knowing exactly where the cryptography stops. Claim the serving stack and request integrity; never claim the model's output is bound.
- ✅ "Served inside a verified enclave. Tampering leaves a mark. Here's the attestation — check it yourself."
- ❌ "Cryptographic proof that the AI said this." / "Tamper-proof verdicts." / "The model provably ran in the enclave."

**4. Theatrical, but dry.** Lean into the drama of the courtroom, then undercut it with a clean, deadpan line. Wit is in the timing, not the exclamation points.
- ✅ "Two stories enter. One walks out with the pot."
- ❌ "Get ready for the most EPIC showdown EVER!! 🔥⚖️🔥"

**5. The crowd is the gallery, not "users."** Address people as participants in a proceeding — they take sides, they call it, they collect.
- ✅ "Pick a side before the gavel drops."
- ❌ "Engage with content and join our community of users."

**6. Kill the web3 slop.** Banned forever: revolutionizing, trustless future, next-gen, game-changing, disrupting, seamless, unlock, empower, leverage, paradigm. We sound like a court, not a pitch deck.
- ✅ "Settle it on-chain."
- ❌ "VERDICT is revolutionizing trustless dispute resolution for the next-gen web."

## 4. The Narrative Arc of the Landing Page

The page is a single proceeding, told in five acts. Each act has a distinct dramatic job and a clear visual break. The rhythm: **summons → the world is alive → here's the procedure → here's the proof → step up to the bench.**

---

### ACT I — THE SUMMONS *(Hero)*
**Role in the story:** The cold open. State the premise in one breath, set the stakes, and offer two doors — file or watch. This is the whole pitch in a single screen.
**Eyebrow / docket line:** `NO. 001 · NIGHT COURT · IN SESSION`
**Headline:** An AI judge that *can't be bribed.*
**Supporting copy:** Two people, one dispute, real money locked on-chain. The crowd takes sides. The gavel drops. The winner gets paid automatically — and the verdict comes with a receipt anyone can check.

*(Full hero copy in §5.)*

---

### ACT II — THE RECORD *(Live docket ticker)*
**Role in the story:** Prove the room is full and the machine is running. Motion and recency do the persuading — open cases, countdowns, fresh verdicts scrolling past. This is the "it's real and it's happening now" beat.
**Section label / eyebrow:** `● THE RECORD — LIVE`
**Headline:** The court is in session.
**Supporting copy:** Every case, every ruling, on the public record. Open cases are still taking sides. Decided cases are final — and verifiable.

*(Full ticker spec in §5.)*

---

### ACT III — THE PROCEEDING *(How it works)*
**Role in the story:** Calm the skeptic with procedure. Show the four-beat ritual so the magic feels like a system, not a trick. Each step is an exhibit.
**Section label / eyebrow:** `THE PROCEEDING · HOW A CASE IS SETTLED`
**Headline:** Four steps from beef to verdict.
**Supporting copy:** No lawyers. No waiting room. No one to bribe.

**The four exhibits:**
1. **FILE THE CASE** — Write the question and the exact rule for winning. Lock your stake. Send your opponent a challenge link; they match it or they forfeit.
2. **ENTER THE EVIDENCE** — Both sides submit their case — statements and links — committed on-chain before the judge ever reads a word. No stuffing the record after the fact.
3. **THE GAVEL DROPS** — The AI judge rules inside a verified enclave, weighs both sides against the rule, and names a winner with cited reasoning.
4. **THE POT PAYS** — Escrow releases to the winner automatically. The verdict mints a card with a wax seal. The gallery's correct callers climb the leaderboard.

---

### ACT IV — AUDIT THE COURT *(The proof)*
**Role in the story:** The credibility climax — and the part that earns respect from a crypto crowd. We hand over the keys and state, precisely, what is proven and what isn't. This is where confidence becomes trust.
**Section label / eyebrow:** `THE PROOF · DON'T TRUST US`
**Headline:** Audit the court.
**Supporting copy:** The loser always says it was rigged. Here, they can't — and you don't have to take our word for it.

**Three proof points (state plainly, no overclaim):**
- **A judge in a sealed room.** Every verdict is served inside a cryptographically attested hardware enclave (Intel TDX). We can't quietly swap in a machine that rules our way — and the attestation report is public. Pull it. Check it against the open-source verifier. Out of band. Right now.
- **Inputs locked before the ruling.** The question, the rule, and both sides' evidence are committed on-chain *before* the judge sees them. The prompt is assembled by a published template — not by us, not by either party. No post-hoc tampering with the record.
- **What we prove, exactly.** Today the proof covers the *serving stack and request integrity* — that the verdict was served by the genuine verified enclave we committed to, untampered in transit. It does **not** claim the model's output is cryptographically bound to the enclave; that's on the roadmap the moment 0G lists an LLM-in-TEE provider. We'd rather tell you where the proof ends than pretend it doesn't.
**Microcopy / CTA on this section:** `Verify a verdict →` (links to a live attestation report)

---

### ACT V — STEP UP TO THE BENCH *(Call to join)*
**Role in the story:** The close. Convert the spectator into a participant. Two clear doors, low friction, a dare.
**Section label / eyebrow:** `THE DOCKET IS OPEN`
**Headline:** Got a score to settle?
**Supporting copy:** File a case in under a minute. Or walk the gallery, take sides, and collect when you call it right. No wallet needed to watch.
**CTA labels:** `File a Case` · `Walk the Gallery`

### 5.2 — THE TICKER / "Live Docket Tape"

- **What it's called:** **The Record** (the live docket tape).
- **Rail label:** `● THE RECORD — LIVE` *(the dot pulses oxblood→gold; mono type)*

- **Verdict chip format** *(decided cases — strikethrough the loser for schadenfreude):*
  ```
  ⚖ No.047 · "Is a hot dog a sandwich?"
  WINNER: @maya  ·  ~~@dev~~  ·  0.8 OG  ·  ✓ TEE
  ```
  Format spelled out:
  `⚖ {case no.} · "{case title in quotes}"` then a second line:
  `WINNER: @{winner} · ~~@{loser}~~ · {stake} OG · ✓ TEE`

- **Open-case chip format** *(still taking sides — a live countdown, an invitation):*
  ```
  ● No.052 · "Did he fumble the bag?"
  IN SESSION · gavel in 02:14:09 · 318 took a side · take yours →
  ```

- **In-deliberation chip** *(the gavel is mid-air — peak tension):*
  ```
  ◷ No.051 · "Who owes who the 50?"
  DELIBERATING… the judge is reading the record.
  ```

*Tone rule for chips: the titles carry the wit; the metadata stays clean and mono. Mix Roast Court ("hot dog / sandwich") with one serious flagship so The Record reads as both funny and real.*
